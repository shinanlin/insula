2. CENTRAL CLAIM AND ACCEPTED ARGUMENT
Working title in current Doc: “Distinct speech functions are spatially organized within the human insula.”
Preferred formulation: “the functional architecture of speech within the human insula.”
Core finding: partially overlapping spatial distributions of sensory (posterior preference), sustained (dorsal anterior preference), and motor (middle preference) temporal response profiles, with task sensitivity, decodable speech content, RT associations and broader cortical temporal-profile correspondence.
Avoid reducing the paper to “insula also participates in language,” a more detailed map alone, or the old anterior-insula cognitive–motor-interface grant story.
Do not make sustained/AMN the entire manuscript; use it to interpret one part of the whole-insula architecture.
NMF identifies temporal patterns first. Task comparisons give functional meaning. Decoding asks what information they carry; RT asks how activity relates to behavior; whole-brain projection situates profiles in distributed activity.
Main Results sequence:
1 widespread recruitment;
2 NMF temporal profiles, rank evidence, spatial distributions, electrode assignment;
3 auditory-vs-picture and decision-vs-repetition task comparisons (passive deferred);
4 phonetic-category then lexical-status decoding and cross-task generalization;

4. MANUSCRIPT-SCALE SCIENTIFIC AND EDITORIAL PRIORITIES
A. Evidence for spatial organization must match title and conclusion.
Results describes maps of weights and regional preferences; Methods describes surface smoothing. Retrieved text does not specify a formal population-aware spatial test.
Clarify whether spatial separation is descriptive or formally quantified, and where sampling density and within-subject dependence enter. If stronger gradient claims are retained, require the actual supporting analysis; anatomical preferences alone are not a measured continuous gradient.
Methods currently omits maximum-W assignment and scaling convention even though Results mentions assignment. NMF scale ambiguity makes norm-normalized H / compensating W scaling important.
Freeze sample flow: total cohort -> insular coverage -> responsive -> NMF -> analysis-specific sites/participants. A full SI table can wait; core denominators cannot.

B. RT is the least finished prose and the largest interpretation jump.
Google Results starts 10707 and 12033 contain duplicated/fractured sentences and repeat the fast/slow observation after the prediction narrative.
Closing claim: “participate ... in the speech planning network” is stronger than current evidence.
Separate:
(1) held-out RT prediction,
(2) post-selection descriptive HGA–RT correlation,
(3) interpretation involving preparation/initiation.
Go-window start<0 does not mean window ends before Go or before articulation. The current Methods explicitly selects predictive windows beginning before Go.
Fixed selected sites/windows in subsequent correlation permutation do not account for the earlier selection. Treat direction analysis as descriptive unless selection is independent or repeated inside inference.
Local method audit: fast/slow waveforms themselves use RT-selected sites. If confirmed for the figure, describe B as illustration rather than an independent discovery that motivated selection.
Local audit says RT training had no universal >2s exclusion, while manuscript says >2s removed; and Delay/Go vs Response were run as separate correction families, while manuscript implies all epochs. Verify actual run manifests before revising these claims.
Do not pool multiple tasks per person as independent subjects. Report the actual summary unit.
Articulatory-category decoding, sustained delay activity and motor RT effects need not come from identical electrodes; do not combine them into one “anterior insula planning mechanism” without demonstrating overlap.

Current auditory/picture Results is still rough prose (“repetition circuit”, “taking input information”) and lacks quantitative effect/statistics/figure callout.

Decision and repetition match input/output modality, not response content, difficulty, or unique cognitive-control process.

D. Decoding interpretation and reproducibility need a focused pass.
Current lexical paragraph (9118) says “lexical-semantic”, “only component”, and sensory effect “due to saying yes and no.”
Word-vs-pseudoword supports lexical-status information, not uniquely semantics. Yes/no is a plausible response-related explanation unless directly controlled. “Only” must be scoped to phase/condition and supported by results.
PS articulatory labels describe phonetic categories; sensory-stage decoding is not evidence of articulator movements occurring then.
Current PS Methods omits cross-subject pseudo-population assembly and missing-data handling. LD describes pooling, but both need actual sample/feature interpretation.
Trial-CV PS vs item-grouped LD support different generalization claims. Across-task decoding does not establish abstract semantics or remove all acoustic/phonotactic influences.
The two decoding tasks and phase-specific site selection mean “same population class carries both” is not automatically “same neurons carry both.”
Phonetic paragraph and section ending repeat nearly the same conclusion; remove repeated summaries after claims are settled.

E. Whole-brain projection and Discussion must retain measurement distinctions.
NNLS projection shows temporal resemblance to fixed templates. It does not measure information flow, causal coupling, or functional connectivity.
Current Discussion (14619) says connectivity differences “leads to” diverse function: use a supported anatomical interpretation, not a tested causal result.
Current projection rationale “due to sparsity ... chose projection” is incomplete; explain why testing recurrence of fixed insular profiles addresses the question.
Spatial projection Methods distinguishes normalized energy-weighted specificity from raw weights; Results/legend should refer to what is actually displayed.
Cross-species connectivity / CCEP / tractography have different specificity; do not compress all into exact anterior-frontal / middle-motor / posterior-temporal one-to-one mapping.

5. STYLE TO PRESERVE
- Plain, compact academic English; concrete observations before interpretation.
- Paragraph sequence: specific question -> minimal design contrast -> finding + figure/statistic -> one warranted inference.
- Discussion develops significance; avoid re-listing panels, stacking citations, or generic claims that all findings “provide a framework.”
- Do not introduce many caveats into every sentence; fix the evidence/claim match directly.
- Avoid repetition of “Together, these findings...”, inflated claims, “fundamental” without content, “cascade” without sequential evidence, and “entire speech process” as universal coverage.
- Preserve phonetic / articulatory-category vs lexical-status terminology, sustained (not sustain in prose), task vs condition, bipolar recording sites vs physical contacts, participants vs subject×task units.
- Results temporal profiles first; independent task evidence next. Descriptive names are not proof of specific functions.
- Established scientific distinction: speech content specificity is different from language-domain selectivity.
- No old grant cognitive–motor-interface recentering.
- Apply user’s requested paragraph/panel order when scientifically coherent.

6. LITERATURE KNOWLEDGE TO RETAIN (previously checked; recheck exact claims before citation)
- Woolnough 2019, https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.53086: high temporal resolution iEEG; sparse anterior pre-articulatory responses, posterior auditory/post-articulation effects. Do not dismiss all previous work as temporally inadequate.
- Kerezoudis et al. published PNAS 2026, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2517734123 (local card still 2025 preprint): simple tongue/hand/foot movements, effector-specific M1 connectivity, insula activation precedes EMG but follows M1. Supports motor involvement, not direct proof of speech planning.
- Berger et al. 2026 J Neurosci, https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1201-25.2025: passive tones/clicks; responsive single neurons more common posteriorly and present in smaller anterior subset. No claim that all insula neurons respond or that single units establish causal necessity.
